## Choice of models — insert in Section 5.2 after the annotation design

The three model families were chosen to span the design space that the method is meant to describe rather than to maximise accuracy. First, each family comes from a different developer with a different training corpus and alignment procedure (Anthropic, Google, Alibaba), so that the item-by-model effect φ_im captures genuine between-family dependence rather than variation among versions of one model. Second, the two commercial models are the efficiency tier of their families (Claude Haiku 4.5, Gemini 3.5 Flash), which is the tier practitioners use for annotation at scale: Carlson and Burbano (2026) report that such models match or approach the larger tiers on binary classification at a fraction of the cost, and cost is what makes the model × prompt × run design affordable. Third, one family is an open-weights model run locally (Qwen 2.5 7B Instruct, 4-bit quantised, served by Ollama): its weights and digest are archived, so this arm of the ensemble is exactly reproducible, which no API-served model can guarantee once its version is retired (Barrie et al. 2025). Larger or reasoning-oriented models were deliberately excluded: they raise cost by one to two orders of magnitude, their suitability for plain classification is unclear (Carlson and Burbano 2026), and the question of the paper is not which model is best but how dependent the members of an ensemble are. Model identifiers, quantisation, decoding parameters, query dates, call counts, missing rates and cost are given in Table [7]; all raw responses are archived.
