\subsection{Design}\label{sec:sim_design}

Data are generated from \eqref{eq:model}--\eqref{eq:re} with $M = 3$ models, $R = 3$
runs, $\pi_1 = 0.30$, $\mu = (-2, 1.5)$, model effects
$a_{\cdot 0} = (0, 0.3, 0.6)$ and $a_{\cdot 1} = (0, -0.1, -0.5)$ (so that later models
have both a higher false-positive rate and a lower sensitivity), prompt effects with
$\tau_0 = \tau_1 = 0.4$, and random-effect standard deviations
$(\sigma_\theta, \sigma_\phi, \sigma_\psi) = \delta \times (1.0, 0.8, 0.5)$. The
dependence level $\delta \in \{0, 0.5, 1, 1.5\}$ is crossed with $N \in \{150, 300, 600\}$
and $P \in \{3, 5\}$, giving 24 cells with 100 replications each. A misspecification arm
at $\delta = 1$, $N = 300$, $P = 5$ replaces the three Gaussian random effects by
scaled Student-$t_4$ variables with the same standard deviations. Every replication has
a recorded seed (data and sampler) and is stored individually, so the study is
reproducible and resumable from the public code repository.

Three estimators are compared: majority vote over the $M P R$ labels; the binary
Dawid--Skene model with $M P R$ raters fitted by EM \citep{Dawid1979}; and CRE-LCM as
described in Section~\ref{sec:estimation}, with two chains of 1500 iterations (750
warm-up) per replication, initialised at the Dawid--Skene solution. For each replication
we record the bias of the estimated prevalence, of mean sensitivity and of mean
specificity (averaged over the $M \times P$ configurations; for CRE-LCM these are the
posterior means of the marginal accuracies implied by the model, integrating over the
random effects), classification accuracy and Brier score of the posterior class
probabilities against the simulated classes, coverage of the CRE-LCM 95\% credible
intervals, and the estimated variance shares \eqref{eq:shares}. Replications in which any
global parameter of CRE-LCM has split-$\widehat{R} > 1.05$ are flagged and their fraction
reported per cell.

\subsection{Dawid--Skene under dependence}\label{sec:sim_ds}

Table~\ref{tab:baseline} gives a first view on a single data set per dependence level.
Under conditional independence ($\delta = 0$) Dawid--Skene recovers sensitivity,
specificity and prevalence essentially exactly. As $\delta$ grows the true marginal
accuracies fall, because the item effects push labels away from the class-specific
propensities, but the Dawid--Skene estimates do not: at $\delta = 1.5$ the model reports a
specificity of 0.82 against a true 0.74 and a prevalence of 0.36 against 0.29. The bias is
monotone in $\delta$ and is larger for prevalence and specificity than for sensitivity,
which the results over the full grid below confirm.

\subsection{Identifiability variants}\label{sec:sim_ident}

Table~\ref{tab:ident} reports the five remedies of Section~\ref{sec:ident}, fitted to
one scenario at $\delta = 1$, $N = 300$, $P = 5$ with four chains of 2000 iterations. With
default random initialisation, one of the four chains of the base model settles in the
merged-class mode (per-chain prevalence near 0.70, class intercepts almost equal, both
between $-0.4$ and 0, model effects sign-flipped); the pooled posterior is a mixture of the two modes and
$\widehat{R}$ on $\pi_1$ is 1.81. The class-specific scale (a) makes matters worse: each
chain ends at a different prevalence (0.17 to 0.51) and the chain with the highest
posterior density is the one furthest from the truth, so the extra loading is not
identified separately from the mixing proportion in this design. Omitting $\theta_i$ (b)
does not remove the mode and biases the remaining components, the item$\times$model
standard deviation rising to 1.06 against 0.80, because the shared item variance is pushed
into the crossed effects. The informative prior (d) changes nothing, the data dominating
the prior. Anchoring 5\% of the items (c) removes the mode entirely, with every interval
covering the truth and the best label quality of all variants. Initialising the chains at
the Dawid--Skene solution (e) also removes it: all chains agree, the intervals cover the
truth, and label quality is close to that of anchoring. Two limitations remain under (e):
mixing on $\pi_1$ and $\sigma_\theta$ is slow, and $\sigma_\theta$ is shrunk downwards by
10--20\% in every chain that sits in the correct mode of every variant, anchoring
reducing the shrinkage. We therefore carry variant (e) forward as the main model and
report anchoring as the practical remedy when a small labelled subset exists.

\subsection{Results over the grid}\label{sec:sim_results}

Figure~\ref{fig:bias} shows the bias of prevalence, sensitivity and specificity;
Figure~\ref{fig:labels} the accuracy and Brier score of the posterior labels;
Figure~\ref{fig:coverage} the coverage of the CRE-LCM 95\% intervals; and
Figure~\ref{fig:shares} the recovery of the variance shares at $N = 300$, $P = 5$.
Tables~\ref{tab:prev}--\ref{tab:labels} give the per-cell values.

\emph{Dawid--Skene.} The single-data-set pattern of Table~\ref{tab:baseline} holds over
the grid. Under conditional independence Dawid--Skene is unbiased for every quantity at
every $N$ and $P$. Its prevalence bias is about $+0.003$ at $\delta = 0.5$, $+0.05$ at
$\delta = 1$ and $+0.10$ to $+0.11$ at $\delta = 1.5$, and its specificity bias
$+0.04$ and $+0.09$ at $\delta = 1$ and $1.5$, essentially independent of $N$ and $P$:
the bias is a property of the misspecification, not of the sample size. Sensitivity is
much less affected (at most $+0.03$). Majority vote is less biased for prevalence
(at most $+0.05$) but has no accuracy estimates.

\emph{CRE-LCM with $P = 3$.} With three prompts the model removes most of the bias:
prevalence bias $+0.017$, $+0.010$ and $+0.004$ at $\delta = 1$ for $N = 150$, 300 and
600 (against $+0.05$ for Dawid--Skene), specificity bias below $0.02$ in absolute value,
and coverage of the prevalence interval $0.95$ or above at $\delta \le 1$. At
$\delta = 1.5$ the bias is about $+0.05$ to $+0.07$, roughly half of Dawid--Skene's,
and coverage falls to $0.73$ to $0.98$. The Brier score of the posterior labels is lower
than Dawid--Skene's in every cell (for example $0.070$ against $0.100$ at $\delta = 1$,
$N = 300$) and the item, model and prompt shares are recovered with small bias and
coverage of $0.80$ to $0.95$.

\emph{CRE-LCM with $P = 5$ at $\delta \ge 1$: a sampler, not an estimator, result.}
With five prompts and strong dependence the CRE-LCM prevalence bias is not smaller than
Dawid--Skene's ($+0.044$ to $+0.055$ at $\delta = 1$; $+0.10$ to $+0.13$ at
$\delta = 1.5$) and the coverage of its prevalence interval collapses (to $0.02$ at
$\delta = 1.5$, $N = 600$). The convergence record explains this. In these cells 83\% to
97\% of replications have a global parameter with split-$\widehat{R} > 1.05$, the
median bulk effective sample size of the worst parameter is 5 to 9 from 1500 draws, the
two chains agree with each other (a merged-class mode is detected in only 41 of 2418
classifiable replications), and the CRE-LCM prevalence estimate is within $0.02$ of the
Dawid--Skene value that initialised the chains and correlates with it at $0.7$ to $0.9$.
The chains have not moved away from their starting point within 1500 iterations. With
$P = 3$ the same cells show the estimate drifting away from the initial value towards the
truth ($-0.04$ to $-0.05$ relative to Dawid--Skene) but still with low effective sample
sizes. The identifiability fits of Section~\ref{sec:ident} (four chains of 2000
iterations, the same $\delta = 1$, $N = 300$, $P = 5$ design) gave a prevalence bias of
$+0.02$, so the discrepancy is a matter of chain length, and the grid results for
CRE-LCM at $\delta \ge 1$ are lower bounds on its performance rather than estimates of
it. Section~\ref{sec:discussion} returns to this.

\subsection{Misspecification}\label{sec:sim_misspec}

% TODO-AUTHOR: drafted by Claude from results/grid/misspecification_summary.csv.
Table~\ref{tab:shares} compares the Gaussian and $t_4$ data-generating processes at
$\delta = 1$, $N = 300$, $P = 5$. Heavy-tailed random effects do not degrade CRE-LCM
relative to the Gaussian case: prevalence bias $+0.034$ against $+0.055$, accuracy
$0.929$ against $0.905$, Brier $0.064$ against $0.083$, and coverage of the prevalence
interval $0.87$ against $0.60$; Dawid--Skene moves in the same direction
($+0.037$ against $+0.054$). The $t_4$ cell is also less affected by the slow mixing just
described (75\% flagged against 92\%). The misspecification arm therefore shows
robustness of the point estimates and labels to the tail behaviour of the random
effects at this design size, with the same caveat on chain length.
