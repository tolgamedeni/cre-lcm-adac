\subsection{Setting and notation}

Items $i = 1, \dots, N$ have an unobserved binary class $c_i \in \{0, 1\}$ ($K$ classes in
Section~\ref{sec:multiclass}) with prevalence $\pi_1 = \Pr(c_i = 1)$. Each item is
annotated by every configuration $(m, p, r)$ of $M$ models, $P$ prompts and $R$ decoding
runs, giving binary labels $Y_{impr}$. Runs are exchangeable within a model--prompt pair,
so the sufficient statistic is the count $S_{imp} = \sum_{r} Y_{impr} \in \{0, \dots, R\}$.

\subsection{The crossed random-effects latent class model}

Conditional on the class and on three item-level random effects, the labels are
independent Bernoulli variables with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:model}
  \operatorname{logit} \Pr(Y_{impr} = 1 \mid c_i = k, \theta_i, \phi_{im}, \psi_{ip})
  = \mu_k + a_{mk} + b_{pk} + \theta_i + \phi_{im} + \psi_{ip},
\end{equation}
where $\mu_0 < \mu_1$ are ordered class intercepts, $a_{mk}$ are fixed model effects with
the reference model $a_{1k} = 0$, $b_{pk} \sim \mathrm{N}(0, \tau_k^2)$ are random prompt
effects, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:re}
  \theta_i \sim \mathrm{N}(0, \sigma_\theta^2), \qquad
  \phi_{im} \sim \mathrm{N}(0, \sigma_\phi^2), \qquad
  \psi_{ip} \sim \mathrm{N}(0, \sigma_\psi^2)
\end{equation}
are mutually independent: $\theta_i$ is the ambiguity of item $i$ shared by all
configurations, $\phi_{im}$ the item$\times$model effect and $\psi_{ip}$ the
item$\times$prompt effect. Runs are conditionally independent given these effects, so
$S_{imp} \mid c_i = k, \theta_i, \phi_{im}, \psi_{ip} \sim \mathrm{Binomial}(R,
\operatorname{expit}(\eta_{impk}))$ with $\eta_{impk}$ the right-hand side of
\eqref{eq:model}, and the marginal likelihood of item $i$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lik}
  L_i = \sum_{k=0}^{1} \pi_k \int \prod_{m=1}^{M} \prod_{p=1}^{P}
  \binom{R}{S_{imp}} \operatorname{expit}(\eta_{impk})^{S_{imp}}
  \bigl(1 - \operatorname{expit}(\eta_{impk})\bigr)^{R - S_{imp}} \,
  \mathrm{d}F(\theta_i, \boldsymbol{\phi}_i, \boldsymbol{\psi}_i).
\end{equation}
Nested cases: with $\theta = \phi = \psi = 0$ the model is Dawid--Skene with $M P$
raters whose sensitivities and specificities are $\operatorname{expit}(\mu_1 + a_{m1} +
b_{p1})$ and $1 - \operatorname{expit}(\mu_0 + a_{m0} + b_{p0})$; with $\phi = \psi = 0$
it is the one-factor random-effects model of \citet{Qu1996} with a common loading. The
random effects play the role of the nuisance-dependence parameters of \citet{Oberski2016},
with the difference that their placement is dictated by the design.

\subsection{Variance decomposition}

On the latent logistic scale the variance of the label propensity of an item over
configurations and runs is $\sigma_\theta^2 + \sigma_\phi^2 + \sigma_\psi^2 + \pi^2/3$,
the last term being the variance of the standard logistic distribution that generates the
run-level Bernoulli outcome. The shares
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shares}
  s_{\mathrm{item}} = \frac{\sigma_\theta^2}{V}, \quad
  s_{\mathrm{model}} = \frac{\sigma_\phi^2}{V}, \quad
  s_{\mathrm{prompt}} = \frac{\sigma_\psi^2}{V}, \quad
  s_{\mathrm{run}} = \frac{\pi^2/3}{V}, \qquad
  V = \sigma_\theta^2 + \sigma_\phi^2 + \sigma_\psi^2 + \pi^2/3,
\end{equation}
have a direct design interpretation: adding models reduces the $\phi$ share, adding
prompts reduces the $\psi$ share, adding runs reduces only the $\pi^2/3$ share, and
$\theta$ is irreducible by design.

\subsection{Identifiability and the merged-class mode}\label{sec:ident}

Two questions arise. Are the parameters of CRE-LCM identified without a gold standard, and can they be estimated in practice? We address both with the scenario-B design of Section~\ref{sec:simulation} ($N = 300$, $M = 3$, $P = 5$, $R = 3$; $\sigma_\theta = 1.0$, $\sigma_\phi = 0.8$, $\sigma_\psi = 0.5$), fitted with four chains of 2,000 iterations.

\emph{Formal identification.} The crossed effects $\phi_{im}$ and $\psi_{ip}$ are identified by the replication structure: each item is observed under $P$ prompts within every model and under $M$ models within every prompt, so item-by-model and item-by-prompt deviations are separable from the run-level binomial noise whenever $M \ge 2$, $P \ge 2$ and $R \ge 2$. The class intercepts are identified up to label permutation, which we resolve by ordering $\mu_0 < \mu_1$; the model effects are identified relative to a reference model. The condition of \citet{Nguyen2023}---at least $2K - 1$ conditionally exchangeable noisy labels per item for a $K$-class mixture---is met with room to spare ($MPR = 45$ labels per item for $K = 2$), and the fixed-effect part of the model is a Dawid--Skene model with a shared logistic random effect, whose identification for $M \ge 3$ raters follows \citet{Jones2009}. The delicate parameter is $\sigma_\theta$: an item that every configuration labels 1 can be explained either as an item of class 1 or as an ambiguous item with a large $\theta_i$, so item ambiguity and class membership are partially confounded. This is the same mechanism that \citet{Qu1996} describe for the shared subject effect in diagnostic testing.

\emph{The merged-class mode.} In practice the more consequential problem was not weak identification but multimodality. With Stan's default random initialisation, one chain in four converged to a mode in which the mixture has collapsed: prevalence near 0.70, class intercepts almost equal (both between $-0.4$ and 0), model effects with reversed signs and inflated prompt variances, all heterogeneity being carried by the item effects. The remaining chains agreed with each other and with the truth. Pooling across chains---as a naive analysis would---yields a prevalence of 0.42 [0.27, 0.74] for a true value of 0.30 and an $\widehat{R}$ of 1.81; the estimate is an average of two modes. This mode is distinct from label switching (the classes do not exchange; they merge) and is not removed by standard relabelling.

\emph{Remedies compared.} Table~\ref{tab:ident} summarises five remedies. Two that seem natural do not work. A class-specific scale for $\theta_i$, in the spirit of \citet{Qu1996}, makes matters worse: with $M = 3$ the extra scale trades off against the mixing proportion and the four chains stop at prevalences of 0.17, 0.23, 0.33 and 0.51, the highest-density chain being the one at 0.17. Dropping $\theta_i$ altogether---using only the crossed effects---leaves the merged mode in place and pushes the shared item variance into the item-by-model component, whose SD rises from 0.80 to 1.06 [0.97, 1.16] and takes every variance share off target. A tighter prior on $\sigma_\theta$ changes nothing; the data dominate it. Two remedies work. Anchoring 5\% of items (15 of 300) with known labels removes the merged mode entirely: all chains agree, all intervals cover the truth, prevalence 0.31 [0.22, 0.38], $\sigma_\theta$ 0.93 [0.77, 1.12], and the posterior labels are the best of any variant (accuracy 0.913, Brier 0.063 over all items; 0.909 and 0.067 on the 285 non-anchored items). Initialising the chains at the Dawid--Skene solution, without changing the model, also removes it: prevalence 0.32 [0.26, 0.39], intercepts $(-2.08, 1.20)$ for $(-2.00, 1.50)$, model effects recovered, accuracy 0.913, Brier 0.084, in 2.4 minutes against 10 for random starts. The base model is therefore estimable without any gold standard once the sampler starts near the data-consistent mode; we retain the Dawid--Skene initialisation throughout and, in Section~\ref{sec:estimation}, integrate $\theta_i$ out by quadrature; anchoring is reported as the practical remedy for users who hold a small labelled subset---the situation of Section~\ref{sec:application}.

\emph{A residual bias.} In every chain that finds the correct mode, whatever the variant, $\sigma_\theta$ is shrunk by 10--20\% (0.79--0.92 for a true 1.00; 0.83 [0.67, 1.00] with Dawid--Skene initialisation). Anchoring reduces the shrinkage to 7\%. This is the confounding of item ambiguity with class membership noted above and is a property of the likelihood at this design size, not of the estimator; the item share of variance is correspondingly under-estimated (0.14 for 0.19) while the model, prompt and run shares are recovered. We report it as a limitation and examine its dependence on $N$, $P$ and the strength of dependence in Section~\ref{sec:simulation}.

\emph{Model comparison.} Leave-one-out cross-validation is not usable here: each item carries $1 + M + P$ random effects of its own, so removing an item changes the posterior substantially ($p_{\mathrm{loo}} \approx 750$ for $N = 300$; 88--93\% of Pareto-$k$ values above 0.7 in every variant). Comparisons between variants therefore rest on parameter recovery in simulation, not on predictive criteria.

A $K$-class version replaces the ordered pair of intercepts by $K - 1$ class-specific linear predictors per configuration, in a multinomial or one-vs-rest formulation, with the same item, item$\times$model and item$\times$prompt effects entering each predictor. Identification follows the same replication argument as in Section~\ref{sec:ident}, with the class ordering replaced by a reference category. We leave the extension for future work; the binary case is sufficient for the application of Section~\ref{sec:application}.

\subsection{Estimation}\label{sec:estimation}

We marginalise the discrete class analytically and the shared item effect $\theta_i$
numerically, by Gauss--Hermite quadrature with 15 nodes, inside the likelihood of each
item; the crossed effects $\phi_{im}$ and $\psi_{ip}$ are sampled. The continuous
parameters are then drawn with the no-U-turn sampler \citep[Stan via
cmdstanr;][]{Carpenter2017} under a non-centred parameterisation, weakly informative
priors ($\mu_k \sim \mathrm{N}(0, 2)$ ordered; model effects $\mathrm{N}(0, 1)$;
half-normal$(0, 1)$ on the three SDs; half-normal$(0, 0.5)$ on the prompt-effect SDs
$\tau_k$; Beta$(1, 1)$ on the prevalence) and adapt\_delta $= 0.9$, with chains
initialised at the Dawid--Skene EM solution.

Integrating $\theta_i$ out is not a refinement but a necessity. When $\theta_i$ is
sampled alongside the class, the $N$ item parameters are strongly coupled with the mixing
proportion and the class intercepts, and the coupling stiffens with the strength of
dependence: in the design of Section~\ref{sec:simulation} the median, over replications,
of the smallest bulk effective sample size among the global parameters (typically that of
the prevalence or of $\sigma_\theta$) falls from 135--290 at $\delta = 0$ to 90--130 at
$\delta = 0.5$ and to 5--12 at $\delta \ge 1$, irrespective of $N$ and $P$, so that chains
of 1,500 iterations do not move away from their starting values. Quadrature removes the
coupled parameters: on the scenario-B data the bulk effective sample size for the
prevalence rises from 13 to about 2,600 per 4,000 post-warm-up draws (four chains of
2,000 iterations), split-$\widehat{R}$ is 1.00, the chains agree to three decimals, and
recovery improves (prevalence 0.28 [0.22, 0.35] for 0.30; Brier 0.059). Each gradient
evaluation costs about five times more, but effective samples per minute increase
forty-fold; 10, 15 and 20 nodes agree to within 0.002 on the prevalence and 0.003 on
$\sigma_\theta$, and at $\delta = 0.5$ the quadrature and sampled-$\theta$ estimates
coincide, so the same estimator is used across the whole simulation study. Two chains of
600 iterations then suffice. The merged-class mode is not entirely removed at the
strongest dependence: in the simulation study it recurs in 8\% of replications at
$\delta = 1.5$, concentrated where the likelihood is sharpest (large $N$, $P = 5$). We
therefore recommend, as a routine diagnostic, running at least two chains and treating a
gap above $0.15$ between the chain-wise posterior means of the prevalence as a
merged-class mode, in which case a small labelled subset should be anchored
(Section~\ref{sec:ident}). This is, in effect, the estimation strategy of \citet{Qu1996}
for the shared subject effect, retained here for $\theta_i$ while the crossed effects
remain in the sampler. Posterior class probabilities $\Pr(c_i = 1 \mid \text{data})$ and
the variance shares are computed in generated quantities; convergence is summarised by
split-$\widehat{R}$ and bulk and tail effective sample sizes \citep{Vehtari2021}. The
Stan programs are listed in Appendix~\ref{app:stan}.
