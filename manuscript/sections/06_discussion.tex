\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\emph{What the variance decomposition is for.} The three random-effect shares answer a question that agreement statistics cannot: where should the next unit of annotation budget go? Decoding runs only reduce the residual share; they cannot touch item, model or prompt variance. When the item-by-model share dominates, a fourth model family is worth more than any number of re-runs; when the item-by-prompt share dominates, the prompt family should be widened. The shared item share is the ceiling: it is the part of disagreement that reflects genuine ambiguity in the text and is reduced only by better coding rules or human adjudication. We therefore recommend that studies using LLM annotation report the three shares with their intervals next to the customary $\kappa$, and that ``temperature 0, single prompt, single model'' be treated as a design with no replication rather than as a deterministic instrument.

\emph{Relation to bounds and to design-based correction.} \citet{Chen2026} show that if the dependence among LLM annotators is left unrestricted, only bounds on prevalence are available. Our results locate the price of that generality: with the dependence structured by the annotation design, point estimates are recovered, at the cost of an assumption (crossed Gaussian effects) that we check by misspecification. Design-based methods \citep{Egami2024,Angelopoulos2023} reach validity from the opposite direction, by spending a labelled subsample on a bias correction (doubly robust in the design-based supervised learning of \citet{Egami2024}, a ``rectifier'' in prediction-powered inference); our anchoring variant shows that a subsample of the same order also removes the estimation difficulties of the latent class model, and the two approaches are complementary rather than competing---one supplies a measurement model, the other a downstream-inference correction.

\emph{Accuracy is not enough.} \citet{Carlson2025} found that a configuration with 87.6\% first-stage accuracy (Claude 3 Opus with chain-of-thought prompting) produced a negative point estimate for a coefficient whose benchmark sign is positive, while one with 55.6\% accuracy recovered the positive association, and attributed such reversals to first-stage error whose direction is correlated with the outcome. Our model does not solve that problem, but it makes its ingredients visible: the class-specific model effects $a_{mk}$ show which configurations err asymmetrically, and the posterior class probabilities carry the residual uncertainty into any downstream analysis instead of discarding it.

\emph{Limitations.} The main model is binary; the multiclass extension is sketched and left for future work. The random effects are Gaussian and crossed; our misspecification arm shows that
$t_4$ item effects leave prevalence, accuracy and the posterior labels unaffected and
bias only the item share (Section~\ref{sec:sim_misspec}),
but other departures (bimodal ambiguity, model-by-prompt interactions) are untested. The shared item scale is shrunk by 10--20\% at $N = 300$ when $\theta_i$ is sampled, which propagates into the item share; anchoring reduces the shrinkage, and with $\theta_i$ integrated out the item share is instead slightly overestimated, by $0.04$ at $N = 150$ and $0.01$ at $N = 600$ (Section~\ref{sec:sim_results}).
In the application the configurations are nearly unanimous, and the likelihood is then nearly flat in the scale of the latent effects (quasi-complete separation): the absolute variance shares depend on the prior, while their ranking, the prevalence and the posterior labels do not (Appendix~\ref{app:prior_sens}); when configurations are near-unanimous we recommend reporting the ranking of the shares, with their intervals, rather than their absolute values.
A bias shared by every configuration---in the application, the LLMs' broader notion of health than the CAP coding rule---cannot be separated from the latent class without a gold standard; a small anchored subset that includes boundary items is the remedy (Section~\ref{sec:app_results}).
Two further limitations come from the simulation study. Mean sensitivity is underestimated in small samples, by up to $0.09$ at $N = 150$ and $0.01$ to $0.05$ at $N \ge 300$: the minority class contributes few items, and the ordered constraint and prior shrink $\mu_1$ towards zero; the bias is conservative, smaller than the Dawid--Skene overstatement in the opposite direction, and vanishes with $N$, so we recommend at least 300 items after adjusting for the size of the minority class. The merged-class mode recurs in 8\% of replications at the strongest dependence level, where the likelihood is sharpest; the two-chain diagnostic of Section~\ref{sec:estimation} detects it and anchoring removes it. Estimation by MCMC takes minutes per fit; an EM algorithm with quadrature would make the method routine. Finally, LLM versions change: the annotations we report are tied to the version strings and dates in the supplementary material, and the model's parameters should be understood as properties of those versions, not of the model families.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

An LLM annotation ensemble is a designed experiment with three crossed factors, and its errors inherit that design. Treating each configuration as an independent rater, as Dawid--Skene does, overstates how good the configurations are and, as dependence grows, biases what they measure. Giving the latent class model the structure of the design yields point estimates without human labels---identification is supported empirically by the simulation study rather than proved---decomposes annotation noise into components that map onto concrete design decisions, and produces class probabilities that are better calibrated in simulation than majority vote or Dawid--Skene. In the application, where the ensemble is near-unanimous, all aggregators label about equally well; the gain lies in the prevalence, whose bias is halved, and in the per-configuration accuracy estimates. The main practical obstacles---a merged-class posterior mode and slow mixing on the prevalence---are addressed by initialising at the Dawid--Skene solution, by integrating the shared item effect out by quadrature, or by anchoring a small labelled subset. Code, prompts, model versions and all annotations are provided for reuse.
