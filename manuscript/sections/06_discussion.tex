\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

[To be completed with the grid results.] Three practical recommendations follow from the
model and the simulations. First, an annotation ensemble should be analysed with a model
whose dependence structure matches the design that produced it; treating configurations as
independent raters overstates accuracy and biases prevalence, and the bias grows with the
strength of shared item effects. Second, the variance shares are a design diagnostic:
when the model and prompt shares dominate the run share, further decoding runs of the
same configuration add little, and the budget is better spent on additional models and
prompts. Third, a small anchored subset of labelled items is inexpensive insurance
against the merged-class mode and sharpens every estimate.

Limitations include the binary outcome, Gaussian random effects (the $t_4$ arm gives a
first robustness check), the computational cost of full Bayesian estimation for large
$N$, and the temporal drift of model versions, which the model treats as fixed. Natural
extensions are covariates on the class intercepts as in \citet{Zhang2025}, ordinal and
multiclass outcomes, and item-level text features that explain part of $\theta_i$.
