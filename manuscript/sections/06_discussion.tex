\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\emph{What the variance decomposition is for.} The three random-effect shares answer a question that agreement statistics cannot: where should the next unit of annotation budget go? Decoding runs only reduce the residual share; they cannot touch item, model or prompt variance. When the item-by-model share dominates, a fourth model family is worth more than any number of re-runs; when the item-by-prompt share dominates, the prompt family should be widened. The shared item share is the ceiling: it is the part of disagreement that reflects genuine ambiguity in the text and is reduced only by better coding rules or human adjudication. We therefore recommend that studies using LLM annotation report the three shares with their intervals next to the customary $\kappa$, and that ``temperature 0, single prompt, single model'' be treated as a design with no replication rather than as a deterministic instrument.

\emph{Relation to bounds and to design-based correction.} \citet{Chen2026} show that if the dependence among LLM annotators is left unrestricted, only bounds on prevalence are available. Our results locate the price of that generality: with the dependence structured by the annotation design, point estimates are recovered, at the cost of an assumption (crossed Gaussian effects) that we check by misspecification. Design-based methods \citep{Egami2024,Angelopoulos2023} reach validity from the opposite direction, by spending a labelled subsample on a doubly robust correction; our anchoring variant shows that a subsample of the same order also removes the estimation difficulties of the latent class model, and the two approaches are complementary rather than competing---one supplies a measurement model, the other a downstream-inference correction.

\emph{Accuracy is not enough.} \citet{Carlson2025} found that a configuration with 88\% first-stage accuracy produced a downstream coefficient of the wrong sign while one with 56\% accuracy did not, because the direction of error was correlated with the outcome. Our model does not solve that problem, but it makes its ingredients visible: the class-specific model effects $a_{mk}$ show which configurations err asymmetrically, and the posterior class probabilities carry the residual uncertainty into any downstream analysis instead of discarding it.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

An LLM annotation ensemble is a designed experiment with three crossed factors, and its errors inherit that design. Treating each configuration as an independent rater, as Dawid--Skene does, overstates how good the configurations are and, as dependence grows, biases what they measure. Giving the latent class model the structure of the design restores point identification without human labels, decomposes annotation noise into components that map onto concrete design decisions, and produces class probabilities that are better calibrated than majority vote or Dawid--Skene. The main practical obstacles---a merged-class posterior mode and slow mixing on the prevalence---are addressed by initialising at the Dawid--Skene solution or by anchoring a small labelled subset. Code, prompts, model versions and all annotations are provided for reuse.
